As we dispatch it

LABSA 90% is linear alkyl benzene sulphonic acid — the anionic surfactant detergent-cake, powder and liquid-cleaner plants buy as acid slurry. At Plot 79/80 we sulphonate linear alkyl benzene with 98% sulphuric acid and dispatch a light brown liquid, readily soluble in water. Buyers neutralize that acid on their line; 90 ± 1% active matter is why a lot from this plant does not force reformulation.

We sell the acid, not the salt

We dispatch the sulphonic acid. Detergent plants make the salt (LAS) on their own line. The 90 ± 1% they start from is ours; we do not sell the neutralized paste.

Where plants put LABSA 90%

Most of our tonnage goes into detergent cakes and powders. The same acid is also specified as a wetting agent and emulsifier in textile, agrochemical and leather formulations. We do not pack consumer sizes.

How it is made at Gundlav

Four steps on the vessels at Plot 79/80. Waste acid from the process is sold to fertilizer plants. Present capacity is 75 MT a day, about 2,275 MT a month on three shifts. Own RKSPL VALSAD tanker and partners move around 50 MT a day; barrels are filled at the plant.

Plant, tanks and equipment

  1. 01
    Charge LAB

    Linear alkyl benzene is held in its own labelled tank. Raw storage on site is 300 MT.

  2. 02
    Sulphonate

    LAB is reacted with 98% sulphuric acid in the sulphonation vessels. Spent acid from this step is sold on to fertilizer plants.

  3. 03
    Separate

    The mixture goes through the separation vessels and aquators on site, then to finished-goods tanks.

  4. 04
    Test and fill

    The lab checks active matter, free acid and Klett colour. Finished LABSA 90% is stored (200 MT finished-goods capacity) and barrel-filled for dispatch.

Yes. We sulphonate linear alkyl benzene, not branched alkyl benzene. Downstream handling still follows the SDS we send with a document request.
